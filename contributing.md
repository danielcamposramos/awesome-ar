# Contribution Guidelines

Please open a pull request that adds one thing, or fixes one thing, and says why in a sentence.

## What belongs here

Augmented reality: registering computer-generated content with the real world, and everything needed to do it. Display optics that let you see through them, the tracking and mapping that decide where the world is, the SDKs and standards built on top, and the history that explains the vocabulary — including where the term came from and who coined it.

The hard parts belong here too, plainly stated: registration error, the vergence–accommodation conflict, field of view, and the safety research. An AR list that only lists SDKs is a catalogue, not a reference.

Dead platforms are welcome and wanted. AR has buried more products than it ships, and saying what happened to each one is part of the subject.

## What does not

- **Web-first AR.** [awesome-webxr](https://github.com/msub2/awesome-webxr) covers WebXR, including its AR module and tooling. Link to it rather than duplicating it.
- **Virtual reality** as a subject of its own. It has [its own list](https://github.com/danielcamposramos/awesome-vr). A passthrough headset that does both belongs in whichever list its entry is actually about.
- **Stereoscopy as a medium** — formats, packings, signalling, stereo photography and 3D cinema — which is [awesome-stereoscopy](https://github.com/danielcamposramos/awesome-stereoscopy).
- Generic computer vision with no AR application, and generic 3D graphics.
- Dead links, abandoned projects with no historical value, and anything whose page no longer loads.

## Quality bar

- **Check the link before submitting.** Every entry here was verified when added.
- One line per entry: `- [Name](url) - What it is, ending with a period.`
- Say what a thing *is*, not how good it is. No marketing language, and no vendor superlatives repeated as fact. "The first AR headset to…" needs a source.
- If a project is abandoned, unmaintained or a service is shut down, say so plainly in the description, with the date where it is known.
- Historical entries should carry a date.
- Never work around a site that blocks automated access. If a source cannot be opened normally, leave it out and say so.
